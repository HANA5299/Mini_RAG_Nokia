1 System concept
1.1 Overview
1.1.1 Purpose
This section provides an overview of 1830 Photonic Service Switch (PSS) product family, describing
the configurations, system profile and network solutions of the system.
This document focuses on the support of 1830 PSS-8, 1830 PSS-16, 1830 PSS-16II, 1830 PSS-32,
1830 PSI-4L and 1830 PSI-8L. The shelves 1830 PSS-36, 1830 PSS-64 and 1830 PSS-8x/12x/24x
are contained in this document in some general descriptions, for example in configurations, as they
are used to build cluster configurations. Refer to 1830 Photonic Service Switch (PSS-4) Release
23.6 Product Information and Planning Guide , 1830 Photonic Service Switch (PSS-36/PSS-64)
Release 14.1 Product Information and Planning Guide and 1830 Photonic Service Switch (PSS-8x/
PSS-12x/PSS-24x) Release 23.6 Product Information and Planning Guide for detailed feature
support on the other shelf types of the product family.
The system supports two software load-lines: SWDM software and OCS software. Each software
load-line may support only a subset of the shelves. In order to indicate under which software load-
line a shelf is operated, the shelf name is followed by the load-line name in brackets: PSS-xy
(SWDM) or PSS-xy (OCS).

1.2 System configuration
1.2.1 Overview
The 1830 Photonic Service Switch (PSS) supports switched WDM (SWDM) functionality and optical
core switch (OCS) functionality within one cluster. The functions are separated into two types of
network elements (NEs), SWDM NEs supporting WDM applications and switched DWDM
applications, and OCS NEs primarily supporting OCS functionality.
• The SWDM functionality is supported by the following shelves:
− 1830 PSS-8
− 1830 PSS-16
− 1830 PSS-16II
− 1830 PSS-32
− 1830 PSI-4L
− 1830 PSI-8L
The 1830 Photonic Service Switch (PSS) product family comprises further shelf types to support
SWDM functionality. For a description of the other supported shelves refer to the 1830 Photonic
Service Switch (PSS-4) Release 23.6 Product Information and Planning Guide and to the
1830 Photonic Service Switch (PSS-8x/PSS-12x/PSS-24x) Release 23.6 Product Information
and Planning Guide .
• The OCS functionality is supported by the 1830 PSS-36 and 1830 PSS-64 shelves. These are
described in the 1830 Photonic Service Switch (PSS-36/PSS-64) Release 14.1 Product
Information and Planning Guide .

1.2.2 SWDM NE
The 1830 PSS shelves provide increased network flexibility and operational automation through
zero-touch, transparent photonic networking. Photonic networks use simplified and accelerated
operations to transform wavelength division multiplexing (WDM) into true transport networking with
advanced flexibility, performance, automation, and integration. Several Optical Add-Drop
multiplexing (OADM) configurations are supported by components that provide optical filter routing,
optical amplification, and support for interworking with optical signals originating on non-1830 PSS
hardware.
The PSS-8/PSS-16/PSS-16II/PSS-32/PSI-8L are closely related shelves that compose the PSS-32/
PSS-16 multi-service multi-reach solution. They are scalable optical transport platforms that
implement a converged platform solution for multi-service DWDM metro-area, long-haul, and
Optical Transport Network (OTN) switching, and leading edge flexibility with next generation optical
and OTN capabilities.
• The 1830 PSS-8 end office shelf provides an 8-slot SWDM platform in 3-RU footprint, with
additional distributed switching functionality.
• The 1830 PSS-16 end office shelf provides a 16-slot SWDM platform in 8-RU footprint.
• The 1830 PSS-16II end office shelf provides the second generation 16-slot SWDM platform in
8-RU footprint (DC variant) or 9-RU footprint (AC/DC variant), with additional distributed
switching functionality.
• The 1830 PSS-32 central office shelf provides a 32-slot high-capacity and high-scalability core
SWDM platform in 14-RU footprint.
• The 1830 PSS-8x central office shelf provides an 8-slot high-capacity and high-scalability
DWDM platform with optical core switching functionality.
• The 1830 PSS-12x central office shelf provides a 24-slot high-capacity and high-scalability
DWDM platform with optical core switching functionality. 1830 PSS-12x is prepared to support
central packet switching functionality in future releases.
• The 1830 PSS-24x central office shelf provides a 24-slot high-capacity and high-scalability
SWDM platform with optical core switching functionality.
• The 1830 PSI-4L central office shelf provides a 14-slot high-capacity compact, modular shelf
type under the PSI branch of the 1830 product family, intended for WDM line system
applications.
• The 1830 PSI-8L central office shelf provides a 31-slot high-capacity compact, modular shelf
type under the PSI branch of the 1830 product family, intended for WDM line system
applications.
The SWDM NE delivers advanced transport capabilities. It is composed of a variety of shelves and
supports converged core transport and switching for wavelength services and sub-wavelength
services such as OTN, SDH/SONET , GbE/10 GbE/100 GbE and Storage Area Networks.

(ShelfId). The shelf number settings are used to unambiguously define:
• whether a shelf is a main shelf or an extension shelf

• a shelf number that uniquely identifies the shelf within the NE

1.2.6 Node Scalability
Node scalability refers to the ability of the 1830 PSS system to combine three or more NEs into one
cluster under one common main SWDM NE. For example:
• OCS NE + OCS NE + SWDM NE
• SWDM NE + SWDM NE
The main shelf of the main NE controls all transponders and uplink cards of the cluster.
On each cluster NE, unique user-assigned NE names for the other cluster NEs that will be
connected must be defined and associated with their IP Addresses.
NEs with optical line resources can perform auto power management for OT line ports on other
NEs. General management functions continue to be performed by the individual NEs which contain
the OT s.
A cluster supports two NE roles:
1. Main NE
This is an NE with optical line resources which automatically manages power settings,
wavekeys, and channel assignments for connected OT line ports located on tributary NEs.
However the main NE does not perform other aspects of management (for example general
provisioning and alarming) for OT s located in tributary NEs.

In order to manage data synchronization between cluster NEs, this feature supports an audit
function that is performed by the main NE against each tributary NE where it has connections.
For parameters set by the main NE on the tributary NE:
• If the audit finds a disagreement, the main NE must re-provision the tributary NE to the values
determined to be correct by the main NE.
For parameters where the main NE only reads from the tributary NE (but never sets):
• If the audit finds a disagreement, the main NE must re-provision its own values to agree with the
tributary NE. The main NE is responsible for setting port-level wavetracker values, power values,
and channel values as necessary for managing transmission on tributary NEs.
The main NE performs an audit/sync against tributary NE data:
• Every time the communication link comes up, including main NE boot-up.
• Periodically, a frequency that does not harm system performance, enables an audit 15 minutes
after the current audit has finished (in normal circumstances, depending on system load).
The cluster audit checks data out-of-sync conditions on existing ports, and fixes the problems
whenever possible. All out-of-sync conditions are flagged by raising local audit alarms, regardless
of whether fixed or not.

1.3 System profile
1.3.1 Key innovations of the 1830 PSS
The 1830 Photonic Service Switch (PSS) portfolio consists of platforms optimized for varying optical
network deployment environments ranging from interconnecting campus data centers to efficiently
scaling large metro, regional and long haul multilayer, multiservice optical networks. Each platform
leverages common software, hardware, management, and control to offer seamless operations
across the portfolio.
SWDM applications
1830 PSS, consisting of the 1830 PSS-4, 1830 PSS-8, 1830 PSS-8x, 1830 PSS-12x, 1830 PSS-
16, 1830 PSS-16II, 1830 PSS-24x, 1830 PSS-32, 1830 PSI-4L and 1830 PSI-8L shelves, defines
the next generation of switched wavelength division multiplexing (SWDM) platform.
• Cost-optimized footprints
• B&W/CWDM/DWDM platform
• Point-to-point linear, ring, and mesh-capable networks
• Fixed/Reconfigurable/Tunable OADM (FOADM/ROADM/TOADM) configurations
• Portfolio of ROADM architectures offering flexgrid colored/colorless, directional/directionless and
contentionless add/drop
• 88/96 channel support
• 1G/2.5G/10G/40G/100G/200G/400G transport support with best-in-class coherent technology
• Single fiber bidirectional transmission
• Service grooming with medium switching capacity
• Interworking with OCS switching for converged WDM/OCS support
• Interoperability with Brocade Storage Area Networking including ISL Trunking (part of Brocade
Certification)
• SAN Certifications across the portfolio for Brocade and EMC Certification Readiness
Note: Starting from Brocade FOS v9.0.1e1: Trunking over DWDM is not generally recommended
due to the risk of out-of-order frame delivery.
Following certifications are executed:
− 8P20 FC1G/2G/4G (ODUk SNCP , standalone, client/line configuration with 11QPA4B,
Y-cable, OPSB_5-single mode optics)
− S13X100E/R FC8G,FC10G,FC16G ISL multiple trunk groups functionality
− S13X100E/R FC8G,FC16 with OPSB5, OPSFLEX and OMSP (50km)
− 12P120S FC8G, FC10G with OMSP , Ycable, ODUk SNCP and OPSB5
− 16P200 FC16G, FC32G cascaded with S13X100 and S2AD200
− legacy OT cards
− Note:Usual protection types for the Brocade certification purposes are OMSP , ESNCP (no
trunking) and Y-cable(no trunking).

OCS application
1830 PSS for OCS applications, consisting of the 1830 PSS-36 and the 1830 PSS-64, defines the
next generation of optical switching platforms for the converged backbone. It provides:
• Two single chassis with 960 Gb/s Tb/s, 1.9 Tb/s, or 3.8 Tb/s full-duplex universal switch
matrices.
• OTN with multiple transport networking options, including Optical Transport Hierarchy (OTH),
WDM, Carrier Ethernet and SDH/SONET .
• Support for any mix of client traffic on line interfaces up to 100 Gb/s.
Client interfaces support Gigabit Ethernet (GE), 10GE, 40GE, and 100GE, OTH Optical Data
Unit-k (ODUk), and SDH/SONET STM-1/-4/-16/-64/-256/OC-3/-12/-48/-192/-768.
• Efficient bandwidth management capabilities at the sub-wavelength level, for high bit-rate traffic
scaling up to 100 Gb/s.

2.1.4 Shelf identification
All shelves in a network element must have a unique Shelf ID set by the hardware Shelf ID switch.
The Shelf ID determines the identity and role of each universal shelf in a cluster.
T o configure the Shelf ID, the 1830 PSS-8, 1830 PSS-16, 1830 PSS-16II and 1830 PSS-32 shelves
have physical mechanisms, called rotary dials, located on the backplane. Each shelf has 2 rotary
dials.
The 1830 PSI-4L and 1830 PSI-8L shelves are not provided with rotary dials to pre-configure the
Shelf ID individually per shelf, on site prior to powering up the shelf. Instead, they use a software
based function to specify and maintain the Shelf ID in a multi-shelf NE. The Shelf ID value is written
by factory with a default into a PSI-L SEEPROM.
Single-shelf layout
A single-shelf network element (NE) consists of one 1830 PSS-8, 1830 PSS-16 , 1830 PSS-16II or
1830 PSS-32 shelf acting as a main shelf. One shelf in each NE has the role of main shelf. This is
designated by a rotary dial setting of 0x81 (Shelf Role = Main Shelf, Shelf ID = 1).
When a shelf boots up and finds that its rotary dial is set to 0x81, it becomes the controlling element
for the group of shelves that make up the network element. The shelf then begins to perform the
role of the main shelf, relative to control and communication functions within an NE.
Note: Two main shelves (that is, two NEs) can not be connected by internal LAN cable as if
they belonged to a single NE (that is, as if one was subordinate to the other).
Main/multiple shelf operation
When used in a multiple-shelf configuration, each 1830 PSS shelf has a Shelf ID number that is
configured through the physical mechanism (two rotary dials on each shelf). The most significant bit
of the rotary dial determines its role (main or extension shelf).
The rotary dial for each shelf must be set to a valid value; this value must be unique within the
same NE. The values of the rotary dial are interpreted as follows.
Bits 4-3-2-1-0 code the Shelf ID Number. An integer in the range 0-31 is possible, but the current
maximum number of shelves supported for an NE is 24. Shelf IDs 1 to 24 are reserved for the
universal shelves, and Shelf IDs 25 to 64 are reserved for the passive shelves.
• Bit 7 = zero >> Shelf role = not-Main Shelf
• Bit 7 = one >> Shelf role = Main Shelf
Bits 6-5 of the rotary dial must be set to 0 (zero).

2.8 Control architecture
2.8.1 Overview
SWDM NEs have a control architecture that consists of a single primary, and single backup shelf
controller, with some control distributed to individual application and I/O cards in the shelf. For
detailed information on individual shelf controller types, refer to “Equipment controllers” (p. 174) .
2.8.2 System control
The active Equipment Controller (EC) on shelves provides all node and shelf control and
management functions and handles the interfaces to management systems. The second EC card is
standby and capable to take over the role of an active EC once the formerly active EC becomes
inactive. Each EC contains a Micro-controller asset (MCA) and other devices to support the
required functionality. The MCA of the EC consists of a processor (CPU) with interface logic and is
integrated into the EC motherboard.
All system components contain a client control function (CCF) that allow them to operate
independently once configured and placed into operation. This function is used for application
configuration / reporting (where applicable), inventory control, FPGA download (where applicable),
and card status retrieval.

2.9 External communications: ECC
2.9.1 ECC termination
The 1830 PSS supports the termination of embedded communication channels (ECC) for OTH
facilities. The ECC frames are transcoded to Ethernet frames for transport between the main shelf
Equipment Controller (EC) and the line cards via the internal LAN infrastructure.
1830 PSS can manage edge NEs using IP protocols through GCC.
The ECCs provide the following data transfer bandwidth:

Data Data rate

protocol stack, which use part of the bandwidth for their own purposes.
A single ECC is associated with a single Network Interface (NETIF). Up to 128 NETIFs are
supported per NE, and among them only 64 NETIFs can be associated with any given shelf.

USRPNL
The 1830 PSS-32/PSS-16 USRPNL supports four MAC addresses stored on its EEPROM. The

There are four ports on the user panel: OAMP , VOIP , E1 and E2. Each of these four ports have four

• VOIP: connection of an IP-phone

The interfaces support a DHCP client role. When configured in this mode the interface will receive
its IP address from the network.
Equipment Controller
Every 1830 PSS-32/PSS-16 shelf contains one EC (Equipment Controller), or two ECs if
redundancy is desired. The EC supports four unique MAC addresses stored in its EEPROM. These
are associated with four auto-sensing RJ-45 LAN ports.

2.11.8 Optical Supervisory Channel (OSC) inter-node communication
The OSC is a separate optical channel, operating at the STM-1/OC-3 rate of 155 Mb/s, that
transfers management and control information between the ECs of two adjacent nodes, regardless
of whether any of the DWDM payload channels are terminated between those two nodes. The
channel is capable of transporting both IP and OSI PDUs.

Duplex equipment controllers
An 1830 PSS node can be provisioned with a single EC or with duplex EC for 1+1 redundancy.
nodes. With duplex controllers, a failure of the main controller will cause an automatic protection

service availability.

management connectivity can be maintained in the event of DCN and/or 1830 PSS NE failures.
Redundant optical supervisory channel

event of a fiber break on any single span.

2.12 IEEE 801.2AB Link Layer Discovery Protocol
2.12.1 Overview
LLDP is a protocol and a set of managed objects that can be used for discovering the physical
topology from adjacent stations in IEEE 802® LANs. It discovers the link-layer connectivity among
Ethernet ports in a network and applies both to physical 802.3 Ethernet (ETY) ports and to virtual
Ethernet ports. LLDP helps to detect and resolve network problems and inconsistencies in the
configuration.
On each port, LLDP can be configured for transmit and/or receive, independently for each of 3
different scopes (nearest bridge, nearest non-TPMR bridge, and nearest customer bridge) using
different MAC addresses as defined in the standard. The 1830 PSS stores the information it
receives from its LLDP neighbors, in the LLDP MIB.
The Link Layer Discovery Protocol (LLDP) is supported on UNIs and NNIs in both access-uplink
and mpls-tp modes on the 11QPE24, 11OPE8, 11QCE12X, Falcon and Sidewinder cards.
Starting from PSE2s, and for all new-generation optical transponders, LLDP can only be configured
for receive mode, and only for one of the three destination address groups (nearest bridge, nearest
non-TPMR bridge, and nearest customer bridge).
LLDP snooping supports multiple (maximum of 10) remote Media Service Access Points (MSAPs)
per destination MAC address.

2.14 Fan control
2.14.1 Control mechanism
1830 PSS-8/16/16II/32 fans are controlled by the active equipment controller (EC) through
backplane links. The EC provisions the speed of the fan motors on the fan unit. A local
microcontroller configures and monitors its associated fan motor
Besides controlling the fan motor speed, the microcontrollers also collect locally generated alarms.
Additionally, all of the microcontrollers have integrated temperature sensors which provide
additional thermal inputs to the ECs.
At normal room temperature the fans run at a reduced speed. With increasing air temperature, the
fan speed increases linearly to the maximum (100 %) speed. With decreasing air temperature, the
fan speed decreases accordingly.
If any one of the fan motors fails, the remaining fan motors operate at full speed. If local micro
controllers on fan unit fail then the associated motor goes to full speed automatically.
Note: For information on the control mechanism for the PSILFAN unit used in the 1830 PSI-
4L/PSI-8L shelves, refer to 2.19 “1830 PSI-L shelf FAN unit (PSILFAN)” (p. 154) .
Fan alarms
For all 1830 PSS fan units, the fan speed is supervised constantly. Any malfunctioning (including
degrading of fan function) of an individual fan element is reported to the user. This includes
malfunctioning where no degradation of transmission functionality is expected and malfunctioning
where the transmission operation is expected to be degraded or interrupted. The alarm contains
information about the degradation of fan functionality, if the system can still operate normal or not
and the failure localization.

2.15 PSS-8 Fan Unit (8FAN)
2.15.1 Introduction
In the 1830 PSS-8 the fan unit is located in slot 14 of the shelf. The 1830 PSS-8 fan unit contains
twelve powerful FAN modules, each individually monitored and speed-controlled by network
element (NE) software.
Note: The maximum power for 8FAN at -40C can be 120W when FAN is set to maximum

2.15.2 Fan unit replacement
When replacing the fan unit in an 1830 PSS-8 shelf, the only fan unit that supplies cooling air for
the shelf is removed. The fan unit replacement is a simple procedure that can be accomplished in
as little as 15 seconds.
If the 1830 PSS-8 shelf is equipped with cards that dissipate:
• <100W per slot and the ambient temperature is ≤25°C (77°F), the fan unit replacement should
be completed within 4 minutes.
• <100W per slot and the ambient temperature is >25°C (77°F), the fan unit replacement should
be completed within 1 minute.
• 100W≤pack<150W per slot and the ambient temperature is ≤25°C (77°F), the fan unit
replacement should be completed within 3 minutes.
• 100W≤pack<150W per slot and the ambient temperature is >25°C (77°F), the fan unit
replacement should be completed within 45 seconds.
• ≥150W per slot and the ambient temperature should be ≤40°C (104°F), the fan unit replacement
should be completed within 30 seconds.
Preparation time and reinstallation time (for loosening/tightening mounting screws) are not included
in the time intervals stated.
Refer to the 1830 Photonic Service Switch (PSS) Release 23.6 Maintenance and Trouble-Clearing
Guide for detailed instructions.
2.15.3 Air filter
Air for cooling the 1830 PSS-8 is drawn through fans at the side of the shelf, passed through an air

consumption card (power>150W), noise level will exceed the noise standard threshold. For further
information of high level power consumption configurations and noise levels, contact Nokia.

and power consumption of the system. SW algorithm continuously adjusts the FAN operation to
minimize the noise level while achieving sufficient air flow and properly take away the heat
dissipated by the inserted modules.

FAN unit protection scheme. When there is one FAN motor unit failure, the system can continue
working without heat dissipation issue; however, all FAN units will run in the highest rotation speed.

• The active S-CRU generates the system clock (T0), which includes the 38.88MHz clock (CK38)
and the 8K multi-frame synchronization signal (MFS).
• The MFS signal shall be phase aligned to CK38.
• The MFS signal shall have the period of 125us for a basic frame and the period of 8s for a
multiframe (64K basic frames).

2.22 PSS-32 and PSS-16 Power filter cards (PFs)
2.22.1 Introduction
Battery plant redundant feeds are brought into 1830 PSS-32 and 1830 PSS-16 shelves through two
redundant power filter (PF) modules. External (typically, battery generated) DC voltages are filtered
by the two PFs and distributed within the shelf by a copper power plane. Each PF provides the 3.6V
service voltage that is distributed by the backplane. PFs also provide power to the USRPNL panel
in the main shelf.
The PF contains a circuit breaker on its faceplate that protects the battery feed. The PF module is
also the source of a shared (Stratum 3 accuracy, +4.6 ppm) precision clock source that is used to
generate the Wavelength Tracker modulation and serve as a high precision clock source for other
transmission-related services.
Note: The PFDCA is the only power filter that does not include a circuit breaker or Wavelength
Tracker capability.
Point-to-point connections from each PF slot to all function slots, as well as the controller slots,
carry this clock signal. Providing these clock sources on the PF eliminates the need to add highly
stable 4.6-ppm oscillators on every circuit card that provides a Wavelength Tracker encode or
decode function. The PF module also contains a temperature sensor that measures inlet air
temperature which is then used to provide input into FAN control algorithm (adjusting FAN speed
based on inlet temperature).
The power supplies are available with, or without, voltage monitoring ports. Power filters with input
power monitoring are compatible with the power filters of the same size without input power
monitoring.
Power filters are available with or without voltage monitoring that provide the following current
capacities:
• 1830 PSS-32
− 20A
− 30A
− 50A
− 60A
− 70A
• 1830 PSS-16
− 20A
− 35A
2.22.2 External interfaces
The power filters (PFs) for the 1830 PSS-32 and 1830 PSS-16 shelves have a single power cable
connector on the faceplate. Appropriate cables are provided with ETSI and ANSI installation kits.
The details are shown in the 1830 PSS Installation and System Turn-Up Guide (ITG) .
